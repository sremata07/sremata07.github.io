## Curse you past me.
Running ESLint on your code wil be demotivating at first. Why should I do all of this, label this and that, put line breaks there, when I understand precisely what I am doing? This project is for my eyes only anyways, so this doesn't matter. This is what I thought initially, but as it turns out, the person you are the day you wrote the code is a completely different person from the person you are when you have to revisit it. Sometimes for me, it's not even the next day, but the next hour where I start to literally lose myself. Lo and behold though, once you get through those errors, I feel as though you understand your own code better, and it will also help your future self understand what you were doing.

## Irritating at first
Initially going through all of the errors ESLint throws at you can be irritating. Some of them overlap with VS Code's errors as well, and sometimes those errors will end up fixing itself later as you continue writing. Plus, sometimes the errors are hard to understand. But the way I've started to see it is like a game. Writing your code to satisfy ESLint on the first go is like winning, no more red errors! And by putting more thought into my code to avoid these errors, I find myself writing not just easily readable code, but also quality code as well. Functions became more concise, less variables were being used, and I could better remember exactly what function I was looking for.

## Then, the easy life
Soon enough you'll find that your quality code has given you a quality life. Now, satisfying that devil on your shoulder named ESLint is an easy task and your code looks better for it. Then you look back and realize how nice everything looks, it's like when you change up your room. Sure, your room before was nice, but just by moving some things around, organizing a couple things, etc., you find that your head is suddenly clearer and your room looks much better and more to your liking now.
